Part 1

Meet the real AlexNet

2 min

PyTorch includes AlexNet already trained on ImageNet. You load it, show it a photo, and get its top 5 guesses, just like Episode 4. Then you look at the filters it taught itself, the same stripes and colour blobs from Episode 3.

Part 2

Build TinyAlexNet from scratch

~6 min

Same recipe as 2012, smaller ingredients: 50,000 tiny photos (CIFAR-10) in 10 classes. The network keeps AlexNet's shape of 5 convolution layers and 3 dense layers, with ReLU and dropout. Expect roughly 80% correct on photos it has never seen.

Part 3

Real-world project: Pet Breed Detector

~5 min

Engineers rarely start from zero. They keep a network that already knows how to see and retrain only its last layer for a new job. This is called transfer learning. You'll teach AlexNet 37 cat and dog breeds from Oxford's pet photo dataset, then launch it as a web app with a link you can open on your phone.

Level up

Challenges

  • Go old-school. Swap AdamW for AlexNet's original SGD optimiser. Is it slower to get going?
  • Remove a trick. Delete dropout or the flips and crops. Watch the gap between train and test accuracy grow. That gap is called overfitting.
  • Unfreeze AlexNet. Fine-tune every layer in Part 3 with a smaller learning rate.
  • Try a modern network. Swap in ResNet-18 (2015) and compare.
  • Use your own photos. Make folders like football_boots/ and trainers/ with 30 or more photos each, and load them with ImageFolder.

Fast facts

The paper
“ImageNet Classification with Deep Convolutional Neural Networks”, NeurIPS 2012, University of Toronto
The result
15.3% top-5 error. The next-best entry scored 26.2%
The network
8 learned layers (5 convolutional, 3 fully connected), about 60 million parameters and 650,000 neurons
The data
1.2 million labelled photos in 1,000 categories, roughly 90 passes over the set
The hardware
Two NVIDIA GTX 580 cards with 3 GB each, 5–6 days of training
The key ideas
ReLU neurons, dropout, data augmentation, and training on GPUs

Words to know

Pixel
One tiny square of a picture, stored as numbers for red, green and blue.
Filter (convolution)
A small window that slides over the picture and lights up wherever it spots its pattern.
Layer
One stage of the network. Each layer builds bigger patterns out of the previous layer's patterns.
Parameter
One adjustable “knob” inside the network. Training means finding good settings for all of them.
Backpropagation
Working out how much each knob contributed to a mistake, then nudging it to do better.
GPU
A graphics chip built for games, very good at doing thousands of small sums at the same time.
